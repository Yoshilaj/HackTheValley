## Interpretation

1. Within-person stable (rel-MAD < 0.15 of the person's median): DFA, HNR, RPDE; noisy (rel-MAD >= 0.30): Jitter(%), Shimmer, Shimmer(dB), Shimmer:APQ11, NHR. Perturbation measures (jitter, shimmer, NHR) wobble by tens of percent day to day in the same person on the same device.
2. A 10-recording baseline underestimates the long-run spread only mildly (baseline10 vs all-recordings rel-MAD medians: Jitter(%) 0.28/0.33, Shimmer 0.28/0.34, Shimmer(dB) 0.30/0.34, Shimmer:APQ11 0.28/0.36, NHR 0.42/0.57, HNR 0.08/0.10, RPDE 0.11/0.13, DFA 0.04/0.05, PPE 0.20/0.27).
3. Within-subject Spearman vs total UPDRS is weak for every feature (|median rho| Jitter(%) -0.02, Shimmer -0.03, Shimmer(dB) -0.00, Shimmer:APQ11 -0.02, NHR -0.02, HNR 0.05, RPDE 0.01, DFA 0.00, PPE -0.03); features whose sign is consistent across >= 70% of subjects: none. Tsanas 2010's UPDRS mapping relied on cross-subject models, not within-person trends over 6 months.
4. Alert rule at k = 2 x baseline rel-MAD in the worse direction: a single recording trips in a median 0.09 of post-baseline recordings (range 0.02-0.12 across features), and 2-of-3 consecutive recordings trip 0.03 of windows - this is the false-alert floor in a PD population with no intervention, before any true change.
5. Implication for PD-03b: use k >= 3 (or 2-of-N with N >= 5) with per-feature k from this table, gate on unchanged recording conditions (noise floor / level / clipping), and treat the low-spread features (DFA, HNR, RPDE) as the primary trend signals; the perturbation measures (Jitter(%), Shimmer, Shimmer(dB), Shimmer:APQ11, NHR) carry Skodda 2013's longitudinal shimmer/NHR effect only at group level and need the wider per-feature k here before they can act as secondary evidence.
